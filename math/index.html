Five Days Are Enough: A Sharp Finite-Sample Gap in Calibrated Forecast Aggregation

Arya Somu · Research note · Draft

A five-observation counterexample showing that replacing binary outcomes with conditional means can change a calibrated forecast-aggregation problem. Three experts force every outcome, while the relaxation guarantees only three correct predictions. The note proves minimality and extends the result to calibration error and repeated observations.

A Hidden Arrow of Time in Fair-Looking Coin Flips

Arya Somu · Research paper · Draft

A stationary process can have exactly fair 20-flip windows and nearly one bit of entropy per flip, yet an arbitrarily large time-reversal divergence rate. The draft derives the sharp entropy threshold and optimal concentration of bias, then certifies discontinuous changes in the optimizer for fair and 40/60 coins. Proofs and exact checks are included; independent review and novelty assessment remain open.

Notes & tricks

Variance of a count

When a random quantity counts events, write it as a sum of indicators. The variance then separates into individual uncertainty and pairwise dependence.

Var(X) = ∑v pv(1 − pv) + 2∑u<v [P(Iu = Iv = 1) − pupv]

Here X = ∑v Iv and pv = P(Iv = 1). The pairwise terms vanish only when the indicators are pairwise independent.

The tail-sum trick

For any nonnegative integer-valued random variable X, count one unit for every threshold X reaches:

E[X] = ∑k≥1 P(X ≥ k).

For each outcome, X = ∑k≥1 1{X ≥ k}. Taking expectations gives the identity. It is often easier to bound the chance of reaching each threshold than to find the full distribution.
