On keeping a notebook

Small observations become more useful when they have somewhere to land.

Most notes begin as fragments. A question overheard in conversation, a number that does not fit the pattern, or a thought that arrives on a walk may not deserve an essay yet. It still deserves a place to wait.

A notebook gives those fragments a second chance. It asks for very little: enough context to understand the thought later, and enough honesty to leave the uncertainty intact.

Leave a trail

The useful detail is often the one that seemed obvious at the time. Where did the idea come from? What changed your mind? What would you need to check before sharing it? A few lines of context can turn an old note into a starting point.

A good note does not have to finish the thought. It has to make the thought possible to resume.

This is also how a longer piece can grow. First collect the observation. Then return to it with a clearer question. If it still matters, give it more room.

Keep the form simple

The page should stay out of the way. Headings divide an argument, links carry sources, and a short quotation creates a pause. The color can change with the subject, while the type and spacing give every piece the same home.
