Reward variation without demonstrated token savings

An audited exploratory study of token penalties in a small coding model. Qwen3-4B selects a repair from four candidate expressions after sampled reasoning.

The completed comparison is small or uncertain. The penalty scores 54/64 versus 52/64, while using 0.72% more tokens. Both principal descriptive uncertainty intervals include zero. One seed, reused development tasks and differing deterministic runtimes limit attribution.

Read paper (PDF) · LaTeX source · Retrospective diagnosis · Diagnostic data

These are recorded measurements, not a simulation or a live model. This page publishes derived diagnostics and four synthetic example pairs; the complete audit archive remains private. Original text, measurements and figures are CC BY 4.0.

Final outcomes and uncertainty

These are final endpoints, not selected checkpoints or a measured Pareto frontier. The task bootstrap conditions on these trained policies and excludes training-seed and design uncertainty.

More update opportunities, uncertain behavioral change

The penalty activates updates in 33 groups with homogeneous success and varying lengths. Success-only has no advantage signal in such groups. Policies diverge after the first update, so later group categories cannot be treated as shared counterfactual trajectories.



Clipping and ceiling diagnostics



All 89 active updates are clipped at raw gradient norm 1 before Adam. Clipping reduces raw magnitude, but Adam's moments make this different from a proportional reduction in the final parameter step. Ceiling hits remain common and do not prove that a longer reasoning allowance would help.
